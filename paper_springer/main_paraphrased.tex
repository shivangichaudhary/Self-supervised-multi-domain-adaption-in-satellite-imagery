\begin{abstract}
Models that apply deep learning techniques to satellite images are often trained based on the images captured using a specific sensor, during a particular season of the year, or in a specific geo-location. It has been shown that the performance of such models declines when the same land cover types are seen in a different acquisition context. Existing techniques of domain adaptation for satellite imaging typically presuppose labeled images in the target domain, a specific predetermined set of domains, or both. These conditions are seldom satisfied in reality, especially with growing databases of multiple sensors images when the ambiguity of domain extremes becomes evident. The study present two components of the proposed self-supervised approach for multi-domain adaptation for satellite images. The first (i) the continuous domain encoder is used for encoding styles based on unlabeled images received from multiple sensors using an InfoNCE-based task. The second (ii) is the conditional generator that implements generational technologies and allows for content transfer from source domain image to an arbitrary target domain image using a single AdaIN-typed neural network that can be conditionally modulated based on the continuous encoding rather than on the discrete domain input. Both networks were trained with a CPU utilizing 1{,}200 patches extracted from the authentic PASTIS-HD benchmark (Sentinel-2, SPOT~6/7, and Sentinel-1 SAR) and assessed using 300 held-out patches from a notably different region. The encoder is able to complete the pretext task; however, its identification regarding sensors is limited (65.8\% vs. 33.3\% randomly). The generator maintains the structure of scenes and smoothly replies to interpolated styles with pairs using SAR, although it fails to reproduce the radiometry of the sensor of interest. A non-varying image matches the actual patch better than a translation does because the latter changes into an independent color. Generalization to pairs of new sensors and downstream accuracy is beyond the paper.
\keywords{domain adaptation \and self-supervised learning \and generative adversarial networks \and contrastive learning \and multi-sensor satellite imagery \and image-to-image translation}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Satellite remote sensing is using heterogeneous optical and radar sensors more frequently than before. Among these sensors are Sentinel-2, Landsat, SPOT, and Sentinel-1. This means that deep learning approaches for land-cover classification and crop mapping must ensure generalization across sensors that differ in radiometric calibration, spectral and spatial responses and noise properties, as it is known that these discrepancies generate considerable differences in images of the same land-cover. Since the process of obtaining labeled data for every sensor is expensive, effective cross-sensor adaptation with little or no labels is crucial.

The existing methods have many drawbacks. Diverse-source standardization techniques like \cite{tasar2020standardgan} function on discrete domain identities, and they cannot be effective in the case of unknown sensor combinations. The self-supervised techniques like SS(DA)$^2$ paper by \cite{zhang2023ssdada} do not require labeling, but they allow for translation between two sensors at once, which results in the necessity to train each pair of sensors separately. While StarGAN~\cite{choi2018stargan} and StarGAN-v2~\cite{choi2020starganv2} methods can be used for many domains of images, they need properly indexed training domains and they were tested with reference quality tests only with natural images. The newly developed diffusion methods given in \cite{vinholi2024multisensor,kieu2025universal} make translation more efficient and improve the transfer of domains without changing the previous classification set and with increased expenses.

As a result, there is no single method for satellite image translation that employs the integration of label-free training, continuous domain representation, and a single generator for any target style. In the following analyses, we leverage self-supervised principles of SS(DA)$^2$ to implement a unified framework inspired by StarGAN version 2 that does not require using the discrete domain approach. We apply contrastive domain encoder pre-trained on unlabeled patches from different sensors in order to accumulate continuous representations of styles and sensing domain features in an efficient manner. The task of translating the source image into the destination one is being solved with the help of an AdaIN-modulated conditional generator that receives the information about the target image through its embedding, thus eliminating the need for applying different models for each sensor pair. The paper elaborates on the design, architecture, and evaluation of the framework components. Generalization to the novel sensor pairs or evaluation of downstream tasks can be found elsewhere, but they are also supported in the provided code.

The contributions are as follows:

\begin{itemize}
\item A self-supervised contrastive domain encoder that learns continuous, binary-free sensor/style embeddings from unbounded combination of multi-image satellite sensor images.
\item Unified AdaIN-modulated generator capable of embedding-conditioned translation without using discrete domain labels or retraining specifics.
\item A combinatorial objective merging adversarial, style reconstruction/perpetuation, cycle consistency, semantic preservation, identity, edge preservation losses to keep geographic framework intact throughout translation process.
\item Activities and evaluation on PASTIS-HD multi-sensor test place comprising images from Sentinel-2, SPOT~6/7 and Sentinel-1 SAR with the results on the issue of embedding quality, preservation of the framework and style effects limitations.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Domain Adaptation for Satellite Imagery}
StandardGAN~\cite{tasar2020standardgan} is used for multi-source condition adaptation associated with VHR satellite images referred to as semantic segmentation. StandardGAN modifies each source domain data to look like one common reference before the application of standard algorithms. StandardGAN is also said to work with a set of source domains in a discrete manner and faced some issues with a strict fidelity of the resulting image content and the possible generalization process with other sensors that were not converted through the known process. SS(DA)$^2$~\cite{zhang2023ssdada}, which serves as the basis for this research, avoids the necessity of labeled target datasets as well as the shape of the boundary between the domains: it utilizes a contrastive, generalized GAN which can translate between two satellite domains without any labels. Its main limitation is that it operates with several pairs of sensors only and for each new sensor introduced to the repository, there should be a new model built.

\subsection{Multi-Domain Image-to-Image Translation}
The first method that brought multi-domain translation under one roof was StarGAN~\cite{choi2018stargan}, which relies on a discrete one-hot domain vector as a condition provided to the network during training as well as inference. StarGAN~v2~\cite{choi2020starganv2} redefines the discrete label with a continuous style code that comes from either a mapping network or a style encoder and is fed into the generator through AdaIN~\cite{huang2017adain}. Notably, the authors claim to achieve much higher synthesis diversity and realism as compared to other methods. Even though both of these methods make use of domain labels in a supervised setting and assess the result using visual quality metrics on natural images (human/animal faces) rather than satellite images or using task-oriented correctness metrics, the authors of other diffusion-based translators claim that the developed models greatly improve sample fidelity~\cite{vinholi2024multisensor,kieu2025universal}. Although higher sample fidelity is achieved, the router in \cite{kieu2025universal} still selects from a list of pre-defined domain experts, while diffusion sampling requires much more computational resources at inference stage than a single feed-forward GAN.

The Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture} outlines two essential components discussed in this paper. The domain encoding system $E$ transforms an input image patch into a style embedding. The content encoding system, which is the downsampling part of the generator, derives a spatial content code. The unified generator $G$ merges the content of a patch from the source and the embedding of a patch from the target to produce the translated image. This generated image is then evaluated by the projection discriminator $D$.

\subsection{Self-Supervised Continuous Domain Encoder}
\label{sec:encoder}
The encoder $E_\theta$ receives an $H \times W$ patch with $C$ channels (4 in this implementation: three optical/SAR-derived channels plus one auxiliary channel, see Sec.~\ref{sec:dataset}) and outputs a continuous embedding $z \in \mathbb{R}^{64}$. After the input-adapter convolution, three convolutional blocks with stride 2 follow (each consisting of Conv--InstanceNorm--LeakyReLU operations), thus continuously decreasing the spatial size of input data while increasing the width of the channels. The SE (squeeze-and-excitation) block~\cite{hu2018senet} readjusts the responses on channel level so that all parts of the encoder pay more attention to the most informative bands (for example, the near-infrared region for plants or VV/VH ratio for SAR).


Instead of simply pooling to a specific class, all first- and second-order spatial statistics of the last map are calculated: the mean value $\mu$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$ for each channel. These two parameters describe both the radiometric and textural characteristics of the patch, neglecting its spatial features.
\begin{equation}
\mu_c = \frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i,j} h_{c,i,j}, \qquad
\sigma_c = \sqrt{\frac{1}{HW}\sum_{i,j}(h_{c,i,j}-\mu_c)^2 + \epsilon}
\label{eq:stats}
\end{equation}
where $h$ is the feature map after the SE block. The concatenated statistics $[\mu;\sigma]$ (optionally fused with a projected acquisition-angle feature when angle metadata are available; this option is unused in the sensor-only experiment reported here) pass through a multilayer perceptron projection head and are L2-normalized onto the unit hypersphere:
\begin{equation}
z = \frac{f_\phi([\mu;\sigma])}{\lVert f_\phi([\mu;\sigma]) \rVert_2}, \qquad z \in \mathbb{R}^{64}.
\label{eq:embed}
\end{equation}

The encoder is pretrained without domain labels, using a contrastive pretext task in the style of InfoNCE~\cite{oord2018cpc}. Two independently augmented views ($z_1, z_2$) of the same patch (random flips and 90\textdegree{} rotations) form a positive pair, whereas all other patches in the training batch serve as negatives:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{con} = -\frac{1}{2B}\sum_{i=1}^{B}\Big[
\log\frac{e^{\,z_{1,i}\cdot z_{2,i}/\tau}}{\sum_{j} e^{\,z_{1,i}\cdot z_{2,j}/\tau}}
+
\log\frac{e^{\,z_{2,i}\cdot z_{1,i}/\tau}}{\sum_{j} e^{\,z_{2,i}\cdot z_{1,j}/\tau}}
\Big]
\label{eq:infonce}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the batch size and $\tau$ is a temperature hyperparameter. Since the positive/negative structure comes purely from data augmentation and not from sensor labels, the embedding space is learned without any supervision of domain identity. At inference time, any patch, whether from a domain seen during training or not, can be projected into the same continuous space.

\subsection{Unified Conditional Generator}
\label{sec:generator}
The generator $G_\psi$ receives a source patch $x_{src}$ and a target embedding $z_{tgt}$, which the frozen encoder $E$ produces from a target-domain exemplar, and it synthesizes a translated patch $\hat{x} = G_\psi(x_{src}, z_{tgt})$. One generator instance is shared across all domains, and the target style is determined entirely by $z_{tgt}$. Translation into any domain that the encoder can embed, and not only the domains enumerated at training time, therefore requires neither architectural changes nor additional parameters.

The content path follows an encoder-bottleneck-decoder design in the spirit of StarGAN~v2~\cite{choi2020starganv2}, adapted to be label-free. A content encoder with reflection-padded convolutions and instance normalization downsamples $x_{src}$ into a domain-invariant content code $c = \mathrm{Enc}_c(x_{src})$. This code passes through four residual blocks modulated by Adaptive Instance Normalization (AdaIN)~\cite{huang2017adain}, which injects the target style at every block:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{AdaIN}(h, z) = \gamma(z)\cdot\frac{h - \mu(h)}{\sigma(h)} + \beta(z)
\label{eq:adain}
\end{equation}
where $\mu(h), \sigma(h)$ are the per-channel instance statistics of the intermediate feature map $h$, and $\gamma(z) = W_\gamma z + b_\gamma$, $\beta(z) = W_\beta z + b_\beta$ are affine parameters regressed from the continuous embedding. The decoder mirrors the encoder with two AdaIN-modulated upsampling stages. It uses bilinear upsampling, instead of nearest-neighbor or transposed-convolution upsampling, to avoid checkerboard artifacts, and it ends with a reflection-padded output convolution with a $\tanh$ activation.

\subsection{Discriminator with Continuous Style Projection}
The discriminator $D_\xi$ is a multi-scale PatchGAN in which spectral normalization~\cite{miyato2018spectral} is applied to every convolutional layer; this stabilizes adversarial training by bounding the Lipschitz constant of each layer. Besides an unconditional real/fake head, $D_\xi$ projects its intermediate features onto the conditioning embedding $z$:
\begin{equation}
D_\xi(x, z) = D_{uncond}(x) + \frac{\langle \phi(x), W_{proj}\,z\rangle}{\sqrt{d}}
\label{eq:disc}
\end{equation}
where $\phi(x)$ is the penultimate feature map of the discriminator, $W_{proj}$ is a learned linear projection of the embedding, and $d$ is the feature dimension. This term penalizes translations whose visual style does not match the conditioning embedding $z$, and it thereby complements the style-reconstruction signal obtained from the encoder (Sec.~\ref{sec:objective}).

\subsection{Composite Training Objective}
\label{sec:objective}
The generator is trained with a weighted sum of six loss terms. Each term targets a specific failure mode observed in unconstrained multi-domain translation.

\emph{Adversarial loss} (least-squares GAN~\cite{mao2017lsgan}), which encourages $\hat{x}$ to be indistinguishable from real target-domain patches under the style-conditioned discriminator:
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_D &= \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[(D_\xi(x_{tgt}, z_{tgt}) - 1)^2\big] + \tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\big[D_\xi(\hat{x}, z_{tgt})^2\big] \\
\mathcal{L}_{adv} &= \mathbb{E}\big[(D_\xi(\hat{x}, z_{tgt}) - 1)^2\big]
\label{eq:adv}
\end{align}

\emph{Style-reconstruction loss}, which requires that the target style can be measured in the output by re-encoding it:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{sty} = \mathbb{E}\,\lVert E(\hat{x}) - z_{tgt} \rVert_1
\label{eq:sty}
\end{equation}

\emph{Cycle-consistency loss}~\cite{zhu2017cyclegan}, which translates $\hat{x}$ back toward the source style and requires the original patch to be recovered, thereby discouraging mode collapse across domains:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{cyc} = \mathbb{E}\,\lVert G(\hat{x}, z_{src}) - x_{src} \rVert_1
\label{eq:cyc}
\end{equation}

\emph{Semantic-preservation loss}, which penalizes drift of the domain-invariant content code between the source and the translation, so that the geospatial layout (parcel boundaries, roads) is not altered by the style change:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{sem} = \mathbb{E}\,\lVert \mathrm{Enc}_c(\hat{x}) - \mathrm{Enc}_c(x_{src}) \rVert_1
\label{eq:sem}
\end{equation}

\emph{Identity loss}, which requires that conditioning the generator on the source's own style leaves the patch unchanged:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{id} = \mathbb{E}\,\lVert G(x_{src}, z_{src}) - x_{src} \rVert_1
\label{eq:id}
\end{equation}

\emph{Edge-preservation loss}, which compares the Sobel gradient magnitudes of $\hat{x}$ and $x_{src}$ in order to keep agricultural parcel boundaries and road edges sharp:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{edge} = \mathbb{E}\,\lVert \nabla_{Sobel}(\hat{x}) - \nabla_{Sobel}(x_{src}) \rVert_1
\label{eq:edge}
\end{equation}

The total generator objective is
\begin{align}
\mathcal{L}_G ={}& \lambda_{adv}\mathcal{L}_{adv} + \lambda_{sty}\mathcal{L}_{sty} + \lambda_{cyc}\mathcal{L}_{cyc} \notag \\
& + \lambda_{sem}\mathcal{L}_{sem} + \lambda_{id}\mathcal{L}_{id} + \lambda_{edge}\mathcal{L}_{edge}
\label{eq:total}
\end{align}
with the weights listed in Table~\ref{tab:hyperparams}. In addition, the framework defines a geometric (SSIM-style) preservation loss $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$, which is activated when the source or the target domain is an off-nadir viewing-angle domain. The PASTIS-HD experiment reported in Sec.~\ref{sec:results} uses only sensor domains, so $\mathcal{L}_{geo}$ is inactive ($\lambda_{geo}\mathcal{L}_{geo}=0$) for the results presented here.

\section{Dataset and Preprocessing}
\label{sec:dataset}
The experiments employ image patches from PASTIS-HD~\cite{garnot2021panoptic,astruc2024omnisat}, an open and real benchmark comprising $2{,}433$ locations of agricultural parcels collected via co-registration across metropolitan France. For each location, an optical time series of Sentinel-2, a SAR time series of Sentinel-1, and in the case of HD extension, a very high-resolution SPOT~6/7. In the accompanying code repository of the study, there is a pipeline that allows for matching patches between modalities based on the patch identifier and exporting the $128\times128$, 4-channel tensor for each modality and location. In the case of Sentinel-2, it is noted that only Red, Green, Blue, and near-infrared bands from one temporal acquisition are maintained. The SPOT~6/7 images are converted from their original resolution of $1280\times1280$ to $128\times128$ and enhanced with a synthetic near-infrared proxy band. The VV and VH polarizations of Sentinel-1 SAR are complemented with a derived ratio image and an averaging image. All modalities are scaled to $[-1,1]$. Up to $2{,}433$ possible locations are converted into $1{,}500$ patches per domain, an upper limit set by the patch cap of the conversion pipeline (Table~\ref{tab:dataset}). These converted patches consist of the first $1{,}500$ patches in identifier order and are taken from three Sentinel-2 tiles. Using an 80/20 split by identifier order results in $1{,}200$ training patches and $300$ held-out patches per domain. Consequently, all held-out patches stem from one tile which appears very little in the training data, such that the held-out evaluation can be viewed as evaluation on another location. The modalities are co-registered but not simultaneous; the median gap between the first Sentinel-1 acquisition and the first Sentinel-2 acquisition is 11~days, while the SPOT images are from 2019, the Sentinel images starting from September 2018. The saturation of the SAR difference channel after conversion is 97.9\%. On-the-fly augmentation of training-split patches includes horizontal and vertical flipping, 90-degree rotation.  and small per-channel Gaussian radiometric noise ($\sigma \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 0.01]$).

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
The framework is implemented in Python~3.13 with PyTorch~$\geq$2.4; NumPy, SciPy, Pillow, Matplotlib, and PyYAML are used for data handling, preprocessing, and visualization. A single command-line entry point orchestrates training: it first runs domain-encoder pretraining and then trains the generator and discriminator jointly. All training and evaluation were run on the CPU of a laptop (Intel Core i7-9750H, 6 cores, 7.8~GB RAM, Windows~11) with a CPU-only PyTorch build. The channel width of the generator and discriminator was deliberately kept narrow (base width 32) so that training remains tractable at this compute scale, and not in order to match a specific reported baseline configuration. \section{Results and Discussion}
\label{sec:results}
All results were measured on the 300 held-out patches per domain with a fixed protocol, and each is a single measurement of one trained model. The style of a target domain is defined as the L2-normalized mean of the embeddings of its 1{,}200 training patches (the domain \emph{centroid}); domain labels and co-registered patches are used only for evaluation. Image metrics are computed on images rescaled to $[0,1]$ against the co-registered real target patch, and two reference baselines are included: the untranslated source patch (``Copy'') and a constant image equal to the mean of the target domain (``Const.'').

\subsection{Domain encoder (Objective~1)}
Table~\ref{tab:enc} summarizes the results for the encoder. It performs a held-out task, solving contrastive tasks (loss 0.24, compared to 2.08 for an uninformative embedding, with 88.2\% of the top-1 retrievals of the second view among 900 patches), and its embeddings reside continuously on the hypersphere, rather than forming three tight clusters. They report moderate, but still satisfactory, performance in describing the sensor: 65.8\% of held-out patches are assigned to the proper sensor, which is not the case by chance (33.3\%); the sensors overlap (silhouette coefficient 0.11); and the effective dimensionality is approximately four. The co-registered patch from another sensor is at chance level indicating that no scene commonality is embedded between sensors. This is probably because instance discrimination is rewarding discriminating between patches and contains no positive proximity terms for the patches of the same sensor; the proximity-based positive pairs proposed in the project plan were not implemented.

\section{Comparative Analysis}
In Sec.~\ref{sec:gap} a proposed encoder-generator pair has already been compared to the closest previous methods in the properties that motivate this work, in Tab.~\ref{tab:gap}. The following design objectives are achieved at the architectural level in implementation: Domain labels are not used in training, one generator is used for all ordered pairs of three domains, the domain code is a continuous 64 dimensional embedding. The quality of the results could, however, not be compared to the cited methods since none of them was trained on the same data and their published figures are based on other datasets and tasks. It is not claimed that it performs better than the other approaches SS(DA)$^2$, StarGAN~v2.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}
There are some limitations to consider in the interpretation of the results in Sec.~\ref{sec:results}. They are held out patches from one Sentinel-2 tile, and training patches from two different tiles; the results reflect the model quality plus a region shift, and in particular the domain centroids are from mostly different regions as compared to the patches they are used for. Secondly, the modalities are not simultaneous, which reduces the level of agreement at the pixel level with the co-registered reference, and the preprocessing generates artifacts (saturated SAR channel and a synthetic fourth SPOT channel). Third, a short number of epochs (10 and 12 respectively for the encoder and generator; i.e. 20 epochs), a narrow network (base width of 32), and a CPU-only laptop (Sec.~\ref{sec:setup}) were used to train each model. There is no training of the training loops, the run-to-run variation is unknown, no ablation of six generator losses was run, and no other translation method was trained on the same data for comparison. Fourth, PSNR and SSIM evaluate agreement to a co-registered image, not realism, and the accuracy of the success rate of the style transfer is evaluated using the same encoder used to condition the generator, which cannot be as reliable. Finally, only three sensor domains are used, one geographic setting, and the $2{,}433$ locations out of the larger MSC-France corpus foreseen for encoder pretraining were not used, and the angle-domain conditioning applied in the framework was not employed. Lastly, the generalization of unseen sensor pairs and downstream accuracy (Objectives~3--4) have not been assessed.

\section{Conclusion and Future Work}
This paper introduced the first two stages of a multi-domain adaptation (MDA) framework for satellite imagery that is self-supervised. The first is a contrastive domain encoder which learns continuous embeddings for sensor style without labels in unlabeled multi-sensor patches. The second is one such unified generator that uses this embedding to translate the content of a source patch to an arbitrary target style, instead of the discrete domain labels that have been used by previous multi-domain translators. The framework is implemented and trained on real PASTIS-HD images on Sentinel-2, SPOT~6/7 and Sentinel-1 SAR, and fulfills its design goals at the architectural level, which are: no domain labels, one model for all ordered domain pairs, and a continuous domain code. The quality of the measured is, however, restricted. The encoder correctly detects the sensor only moderately (65.8\% compared to 33.3\% chance) and the generator preserves the structure of the scene and also uses the style vector, but it doesn't reproduce the radiometry of the target sensor, translations into Sentinel-2 do not succeed.

Future work will further build on these results, by training wider networks on a GPU for longer, using the data split by domain/tile, and by using temporally matched acquisitions and a non-saturating SAR channel to correct the data issues identified in this work; and finish the evaluation of the leave-one-domain-pair-out and zero-shot (SpaceNet~6) generalization (Objectives~3 and~4 of the overall project), including the downstream evaluation of the crop-classification and segmentation task.
